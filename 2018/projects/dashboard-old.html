Background

As a campaign manager, the goal of sending out a campaign is to onboard the users, drive the engagement, and even retain the users. After the campaign launched, the marketers used the analytics tool to see how their campaign performed and used this data to help them determine the marketing strategy in the future.

In this project, my goal was to redesign the performance dashboard and create a better dashboard experience. My role was from end to end defining the problem, doing user research, creating mocks, testing and pairing with engineers to implement the final design.

User Research

When this project started, the design team also had a side project came in, which was persona research. We categorize our users into three group: Campaign Manager, Head of Marketing, and Engineer. During the interview, we found that both Campaign Manager and Head of Marketer were the audiences of Campaign Performance Dashboard.

“We have different goals for different campaigns, but I mostly care about the conversion rate.”

- Head of Marketing Manager

We interviewed 6 Campaign Managers and 7 Head of Marketing. Based on the research collected, we created our target persona: Campaign Manager.

The goal of our persona research was to find out the marketer’s main goal and the pain points that in the current industry and process. Through the interview, our team was looking for how they monitor the results and understand what's the most frustrating part of this process.

  • Head of Marketers cared more about high-level metrics; Campaign Managers looked into the data to find out opportunity.

User Scenario

After the research, we referred back to our target users, Campaign Manager and Head of Marketer. Creating a scenario for each persona. We want to find out the key element in specific needs and figure out the flow for them.

For Catherine: “Catherine takes an active role in crafting, coordinating, targeting, launching, and analyzing marketing campaigns. After a period of time, she needs to analyze the campaign results and reports to her boss. She also looks into the data from the past campaigns and uses it to create hypotheses for new ones.”

Key points to take from Catherine’s scenario:

  • Catherine needs to analyze the campaign in an aggregate level.
  • She checks the metrics to see how the campaigns run overall and in detail.
  • She deep dive into the data from the campaign to find out the useful information.

For Daniel: “Daniel sets goals for marketing campaigns to drive customer acquisition and engagement. He analyzes data to find the insights that can be tied back to business metrics to drive future strategies. He also presents key findings to the executive team.”

Key points to take from Daniel's scenario:

  • Daniel looks for the high-level metrics, such as engagement, goal achieve, or conversion
  • He looks into the data to map back the business goal.

Project Goal

One of our business goals is to grow our channels in the nearly future, but most of the current interface didn’t plan for a growing feature set. We wanted to start from this dashboard and apply it across to all analytics page.

Considering the current issues, user’s workflow and our business goals, we had a lot of room to improve. But we knew that time was always limited. So we had to define & prioritize specific things we want to improve.

Based on our research, we decided on a list of “Must-Have” improvement:

  • Eliminate all the confusing interactions, such as togglable KPI tiles.
  • Clean up the filter.
  • Scalability. Consider all the components and find out a better solution if the content starts to grow.
  • Redesign the campaign list view.

Design

Part 1: Where does filter live?

According to our persona research and customer feedbacks, one of the campaign manager’s goal is to review the performance of campaigns and use the data to drive future campaigns. However, in our current dashboard, we took too much space to display filter selection, which is not really effective.

Current Screen - Take first two row displaying the filter setting.

So I brainstormed with different ideas on the paper and shared with the team:

Filter Idea Sketch

Then collecting feedbacks from PM and another designer. I evaluated pros and cons of each design.

01

Push the content down when clicking filter button.

Pros:

  • Save space when the panel collapse.

Cons:

  • Take too much space when opening.
  • The content will be pushed while open the panel.
  • The users won't know the setting once they close the panel.

02

Open the filter as a popup panel.

Pros:

  • Save space.

Cons:

  • The users won't know the setting once they close the panel.
  • The position of the filter is not right. Changing the setting will affect the whole page. (It can be solved by placing the button on the top)

03

Fixed the panel on the side.

Pros:

  • Clear to see the filter setting.
  • Easy to adjust the filter.

Cons:

  • Take more sapce.

We decided to go with the third solution. The reason was although hiding filters can save more space, the users still need a way to know what’s the filter setting. The advantage of fixing the panel on the one side is it’s easier to adjust the filter and view the result directly.

Part 2: Exploring The KPI Tiles

Togglable KPI?

Then we discovered the interaction across all the dashboard, we found that in some of the dashboard, users can click the KPI tiles to interact with the chart, but some of can’t. We've also had the data showing that more than 50% of the users didn't know that the KPI tiles are toggles when first used.

Engagement Trend KPI Tiles - not consistent of interaction behavior

How to scale?

The other problem was when we keep growing our numbers of channel how the metrics can be scaled and how to show the breakdown of channels.

The other concern was how many metrics we should show on the KPI tile? Because eventually we might have more than three channels, and some of the metrics only for one specific channel, such as Unsubscribe is only by email.

We went through several iterations at this part. The final solution I proposed was we only show the high-level KPIs and group the negative metrics into one metric, called “attritions” and had a toggle to show/hide the channel breakdown for each metric.

If the users want to deep dive into detail, they can go to the specific campaign to see those metrics.
